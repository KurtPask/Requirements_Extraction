1. Purpose.  To establish policy for planning, determining and documenting manpower, 
personnel and training (PMT) requirements and developing of professional, technical and 
leadership training for Navy and Navy and Marine Corps integrated weapons and warfare 
systems across the continuum of Naval training (apprentice, journeyman, and master level) 
throughout the system’s life cycle.  This revision aligns with changes in policy, procedures, 
responsibilities and reporting identified in reference (a), missions, functions and tasks published 
in reference (b) and additional guidelines established in the references identified in enclosure (1).  
This instruction is a complete revision and should be reviewed in its entirety. 
 
2. Cancellation.  OPNAVINST 1500.76D. 
 
3. Scope and Applicability.  This instruction applies to Navy and Marine Corps Defense System 
programs, as identified in references (c), (d), (e) and (f).  Specific Department of Defense (DoD) 
instructions or directives for guidance and management can be found under references (c), (g), 
(h), (i) and (j) for major capability programs, middle tier and software programs.  Additionally, 
this instruction applies to modernization programs.  Joint and Inter-Service training programs 
will follow the procedures outlined in references (d) and (f).  This instruction facilitates the 
alignment of the system, manpower and training requirements throughout the lifecycle of the 
system and rating continua.  This instruction also serves as guidance for the Department of the 
Navy (DON) in building training effectiveness assessment models.  Compliance with this policy  
will ensure PMT requirements traceability in support of new or modernized (or both) Navy 
training capabilities via the enclosures contained in this instruction.

4. Background.  This instruction requires the identification of total force manpower and training 
requirements and the development of a training sustainment plan for support of Navy and 
integrated Navy and Marine Corps or modernization programs.  Navy training system plans 
(NTSP) are regulatory documents identified by reference (e).  References (e) and (f) provide 
procedural guidance for the Joint Capabilities Integration and Development System (JCIDS) as 
the primary means for the Joint Requirements Oversight Council (JROC) to fulfill its statutory 
responsibilities.  This instruction includes assessing joint military capabilities and identifying, 
approving and prioritizing gaps in these capabilities to meet requirements in the National 
Defense Strategy.  References (k) and (l) address the requirements development for human 
systems integration. 
 
5. Policy 
 
 
a. Manpower and Training Requirements Planning (MTRP).  The MTRP process identifies 
the strategy and tasks associated with developing manpower and training requirements and 
integrating them into warfare system design, development, testing, fielding and operations.  
Front end analyses (FEA) are a primary component of MTRP and are comprised of the baseline 
comparison system (BCS) analysis, the task analysis, the training systems requirements analysis 
(TSRA) and the rating domain analysis (RDA) and career flow map (CFM).  The TSRA is a 
structured process used to examine training requirements and identify alternative approaches to 
training job tasks.  The TSRA is comprised of the training situation analysis as documented in 
the training situation document (TSD), the Task and Learning Analysis as documented in the 
instructional performance requirements document (IPRD), the media selection as documented in 
the instructional media requirements document (IMRD), and the output of the Navy’s 
Assessment, Analysis and Evaluation (AAE).  A technical assessment of potential recommended 
training systems, a complete description of the alternatives (including costing data for 
development, implementation and sustainment) and a recommended solution with supporting 
rationale is documented in the training decision coordinating paper (TDCP).  The military 
characteristics document (MCD) discusses how the training device (TD) integrates with the 
training system to fulfill the training requirements outlined in the TSRA and TDCP.  TSRA 
results inform Program Office, Naval Education and Training Command (NETC) and Type 
Commander (TYCOM) training plans in alignment with ready for training (RFT) and follow-on 
training execution in the Fleet.  Revisions to the TSRA associated documents will be included 
in a revised NTSP or in a revised FEA as applicable at the program manager’s (PM) discretion.  
The MTRP analysis should utilize references (f), (m) and (n) to post findings in official 
repositories and be completed as identified in enclosure (2). 
 
 
b. Training Estimate (TE).  The TE describes the PMT requirements and funding across the 
Future Years Defense Program (FYDP).  The acquiring organization will provide the identified 
training and produce a TE and total life-cycle cost estimate (TLCE) to support the production 
and development phase requirements.  Urgent or joint emergent needs, modernization and rapid 
or abbreviated programs, including non-developmental items, commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS), 
government-off-the-shelf (GOTS) rapid deployment capability recommended for the transition 
into a program of record, will be incorporated into a TE and TLCE and a follow-on NTSP.  The 
TE must be completed prior to capability design review.  The completion of the TE and TLCE is 
the entrance criteria for the FEA documents that provide content for the full development of an

c. NTSP.  The NTSP is required for integrated weapons and warfare systems and functional 
areas (e.g., habitability, damage control, etc.).  An NTSP applies to equipment, systems and 
subsystems (ESS) or to a larger scale such as a ship platform.  The NTSP is the output of MTRP.  
The MTRP consists of the items in Exhibit 2-1 of enclosure (2) with the applicable product 
support analysis data, life-cycle management data, maintenance task analysis, credential 
transparency description language and personnel qualifications standards (PQS).  All NTSPs 
must state whether the NTSP capability is an acquisition category (ACAT) I through IV program 
of record, a non-ACAT capability or rapid acquisition program.  The PM must complete an 
annual review of the NTSP to ensure the operational and sustainment requirements are met as 
identified in enclosures (2) and (3). 
 
 
d. AAE.  The AAE describes the strategy, goals and objectives to conduct assessment, 
analysis and evaluation.  The AAE uses AAE Business Rule 1 for NETC training and Business 
Rule 2 for all other solutions that identify methodologies to collect and evaluate data of ESS 
training systems and other requirements that improve readiness as identified in enclosure (4).   
Updates will be developed in line with MTRP triggers (see Exhibit 2-1 of enclosure (2)).  For 
further guidance refer to the AAE Strategy, Process, Standard Operating Procedures and 
Business Rules located at:  https://flankspeed.sharepoint-
mil.us/sites/MyNavyHR_Communities/OPNAV_N13M5. 
 
 
e. Training Installation and Transfer Agreement (TITA).  The TITA describes training 
products, equipment, facilities and infrastructure requirements that need to be in place to ensure 
sustainment of the training system throughout the life of the requirement.  Approval of the TITA 
shifts responsibility for complete training systems delivered by the Training Support Agency 
(TSA) to the identified training agency (TA).  The TITA is required for all ACAT programs, 
Fleet and NETC training solutions and may be tailored to the needs of the PM.  The TITA must 
be completed and approved before the RFT date and as identified in enclosure (5). 
 
 
f. Rating Continuum.  Office of the Chief of Naval Operations (OPNAV), Director, 
Military Personnel Plans and Policy (OPNAV N13) ensures completion of RDA with the output 
of a Rating Continuum or CFM.  All Navy enlisted classifications (NEC) are organized 
according to their career fields.  The foundation for a rating continuum is the technical, 
professional and leadership requirements as plotted on each ratings’ CFM and supported by tasks 
identified in the rating career domain continuum (RCDC) and the training and education needed 
throughout a career.  The Rating Continuum must be completed and approved as identified in 
reference (o) and enclosure (6).  For the listing of ratings and associated stakeholders, refer to the 
Ready Relevant Learning (RRL) Matrix located at:  https://flankspeed.sharepoint-
mil.us/sites/MyNavyHR_Communities/OPNAV_N13M5.

MANPOWER AND TRAINING REQUIREMENTS PLANNING 
 
1. Introduction.  MTRP communicates the “who,” “what,” “where,” “when,” and “how” 
strategy needed to develop requirements and integrate sustainment requirements into system 
design, development, testing, fielding and operations and support.  The task-based approach to 
manpower and training requirements determination provides PMs with the flexibility to tailor the 
level of PMT analysis to their program.  In support of this effort, this enclosure (2) guides the 
production and sustainment of the MTRP to meet the user’s operational needs, lifecycle, 
performance support and the estimation of total ownership costs.  The focus is to maintain the 
readiness of a system and describe the role the human plays to make the system mission capable.  
This enclosure (2) supports RS planning and programming of requirements and resources 
necessary to deliver effective integrated logistics products supporting integrated weapons and 
warfare system readiness.  The analyses consider all interventions to improve the system and 
human performance to satisfy training requirements and align to reference (n) NTP. 
 
2. Purpose.  To document the MTRP conducted analyses that identify the requirements, 
proposed training system solution(s) and manpower required to operate and maintain ESS.   
 
3. Process.  The MTRP is task-based to ensure production and sustainment meet the user’s 
operational needs, life cycle, performance support and the estimation of total ownership costs. 
 
 
a. Although a significant amount of data types are common between ESS, there are distinct 
differences in detail and scope.  There is also a significant difference in detail and scope between 
ESS and platforms.  Additionally, there is a significant amount of ESS Government Furnished 
Information required to track, monitor and ensure deliveries to support alignment with 
requirements. 
 
 
b. During the Material Solution Analysis phase, the BCS Analysis specifies assessment of 
mission effectiveness, operational suitability and estimated lifecycle costs of alternative solutions 
based on system design specifications.  During engagements with stakeholders, logistics 
supportability, configuration management, materiel options and limitations of future training for 
Abbreviated Acquisition Programs and Rapid Acquisition Program will be assessed to establish 
any triggers for execution.  The BCS Analysis establishes the foundational starting point for 
follow-on PMT requirements analysis, development and assessment efforts.  The BCS Analysis 
also ensures that PMT stakeholders use the same set of baseline data when subsequently 
considering and evaluating new, modified or updated system(s) training requirements and 
alternatives.   
 
 
c. Each PM should tailor their process to ensure that training is included in the development 
of the intellectual property strategy.  The process should include the development and 
management of the Crew Scheduling and Phasing Plan.

(d) BCS Analysis 
 
 
 
 
 
1. Introduction.  The BCS Analysis establishes the foundational starting point for 
follow-on PMT requirements analysis, development and assessment efforts.  The BCS Analysis 
also ensures PMT Stakeholders use the same set of baseline data when evaluating new or 
modified system(s) training requirements and alternatives.  The BCS Analysis utilizes an 
existing system to define a quantitative baseline for training and manpower projections for the 
new or modified warfare system as a starting point for subsequent analysis.  The BCS Analysis is 
a method for estimating manpower and training impacts and resource requirements early in a 
system's lifecycle, with the goal of establishing TDs, equipment and facility installation 
requirements and integrated product data.  Additionally, the BCS Analysis provides a task 
analysis of an existing system for use in the examination of alternative technologies and 
engineering solutions.  The application of this process ensures integration of PMT into the design 
effort of the warfare system to improve performance and reduce lifecycle costs.

(3) TSRA 
 
 
 
 
(a) Introduction. TSRA is a sequenced set of analyses that identifies the basic 
purpose of the training ESS, type of training provided, location and performance requirements, 
recommends appropriate learning objectives with recommended training strategies for new or 
modified training requirement(s).  The TSRA determines the “what, when and how” to deliver 
training.  The TSRA defines training requirements (e.g., training tasks, learning objectives, 
instructional strategies, instructional methods, media selection, throughput and facilities) for 
new or modified training, provides rationale for developing TDs and identifies training 
development and implementation risks.  The TSRA includes the Training Situation Analysis 
documented in a TSD, Task and Learning Analyses documented in an IPRD and Media 
Selection documented in an IMRD.  A technical assessment of potential training systems 
recommended and a complete description of the alternatives (including costing data for 
development, implementation and sustainment) and a recommended solution with supporting 
rationale is documented the TDCP.  These documents depict the delta between the “as-is” and 
“to-be” comparison in order to conduct a TSRA.  The results of the TSRA analyses inform the 
Program Office, NETC and TYCOM training plans and delivery in alignment with RFT and 
follow-on performance in the Fleet.  For legacy systems where an NTSP already exists, 
revisions to the TSRA Task and Learning Analyses, Media Selection and MCD will be included 
in the revised NTSP or in a revised FEA TSRA document.  The determination of which analyses 
should be the starting point is based on prior analyses, existing data and need.

13.4 
Phase 1 and Phase 2 testing includes facility power verification (voltage and phase), 
power cable verification, pre-equipment installation tests, hydrostatic pressure tests, cleaning 
tests, power or water or hydraulic interface validation tests and cable continuity and insulation 
resistance tests.  It also includes the external examination of equipment before any power being 
applied to the equipment.  If the external examination shows the potential for internal damage, 
the TSA will be notified. 
 
 
13.5 
Phase 3 testing includes equipment check-out, grooming and post-installation testing.  
It provides for power application to equipment and the performance of all operability testing 
necessary to ensure that a subsystem or standalone trainer (or both) are operating according to 
trainer or trainer system specifications.  This testing will include system-level operability tests to 
verify that an entire trainer system, including ESS and multi-trainer interfaces, operates and 
performs as a complete and composite system within tolerances and parameters specified in the 
governing trainer system specifications. 
 
 
13.6 
Phase 4 certification and acceptance testing consists of portions of the equipment 
check out and groom tests that demonstrate trainer performance within the approved design and 
performance specifications.  This testing is a prerequisite for the turnover of the trainer to the 
learning facility.

knowledge and skills for career development and advancement.  A rate identifies personnel

focuses on “what” tasks (system and rating), skills, certifications, qualifications and education 
outcomes are required of a sailor during a career.  Rating continuums are multi-phased, 
sequenced products that align Navy ratings in career fields and broad communities providing a 
foundation for future development and more flexible and sustainable career paths for sailors.  
Rating continuums include the technical, professional and leadership tasks required of a sailor 
during a career as depicted in reference (p).  The following Exhibit 6-1 shows the Product Roles

GLOSSARY AND ACRONYMS 
 
1. AAE.  The AAE provides the strategy, process, and standard operating procedure for 
evaluating training Business Rule 1 and performance Business Rule 2. For further guidance refer 
to AAE Business Rules located at: https://flankspeed.sharepoint-
mil.us/sites/MyNavyHR_Communities/OPNAV_N13M5. 
 
2. Acquisition Strategy Document.  A business and technical management approach designed to 
achieve program objectives within the resource constraints imposed.  It is the framework for 
planning, directing contracting for and managing a program. 
 
3. Analysis of alternatives.  The evaluation of the performance, operational effectiveness, 
operational suitability and estimated costs of alternative systems to meet a mission capability.  
The analysis of alternatives assesses the advantages and disadvantages of alternatives being 
considered to satisfy capabilities, including the sensitivity of each alternative to possible changes 
in key assumption or variables.  The analysis of alternatives is one of the key inputs to defining 
the system capabilities in JCIDS. 
 
4. BCS.  A current operational system or a composite of current operational subsystems, which 
most closely represents the design, operational and support characteristics of the new system 
under development and which possesses mature manpower and training data. 
 
5. Billet Training Profile.  Documents-specific training required for each billet, including 
course and training description, periodicity, course number, reference and training points. 
 
6. Business Case Analysis.  The Business Case Analysis provides a cost analysis for the 
proposed training systems from the MTRP.  The Business Case Analysis also captures any risk 
associated with each option.  NETC will propose training systems, as a course of action for each 
training system, to the requirement sponsor and RS.  Collectively, these entities will select one of 
the training systems courses of action that NETC can then use to develop or revise a course to 
satisfy the training requirement.  It is the output of the business case analysis (decisions by the 
requirement sponsor and RS) that will determine the sand knowledge outcomes that the course 
will be developed to achieve, and tests built to assess. 
 
7. Career Domain Analysis.  Will include the rating master task list and CFM, connecting all 
training to applicable task requirements (RCDC), contain a priority of implementation with cost 
order of magnitude data to execute POM. 
 
8. CFM.  Graphically illustrates all rating-level requirements year zero across a 30-year career 
addressing leadership, professional and technical requirements. 
 
9. Configuration.  A collection of an item’s descriptive and governing characteristics, which can 
be expressed in functional terms, (e.g., what performance the item is expected to achieve) and in

campaign plans and operation plans; in the latter case, mainly when the plans cover a series of

37. MOS.  The duty or related group of duties that a soldier or marine by training, skill and 
experience is best qualified to perform and that is a basis for the classification, assignment and 
advancement of enlisted personnel.  MOSs are divided into Primary Military Occupational 
Specialties (PMOS) and non-primary MOSs. PMOS is used to identify the primary skills and 
knowledge of a marine.  Changes to an active component Marine's PMOS without approval from 
Commandant of the Marine Corps and changes to a reserve component marine's PMOS without 
approval from Commandant of the Marine Corps resource advocates are not authorized.  There 
are three types of non-PMOSs:  1) Necessary MOS) – A non-PMOS with a prerequisite of one or 
more PMOSs.  This MOS identifies a particular skill or training in addition to a marine's PMOS 
but can only be filled by a marine with a specific PMOS.  When entered as a requirement into the 
TFSMS, a billet bearing a necessary MOS must identify a single associated PMOS even if 
several PMOSs are acceptable prerequisites; 2) Free MOS – Non-PMOS that can be filled by any 
marine regardless of primary MOS.  A free MOS requires skillsets unrelated to primary skills; 
and 3) Exception. 
 
38. MS.  Major decision points that separate the phases of a program. 
 
39. MTRP.  A collection of discreet requirement determination tasks resulting in the 
development of the technical program data, part I of the NTSP, the output of which is included in 
the TE.  Part I states the underlying concepts that will govern the operation, maintenance, 
training, the quantity and quality of personnel required and the necessary training hardware.  
MTRP is designed to provide PMT analysts with a systematic set of tasks that will determine the 
best possible PMT profile of an initial acquisition or allow examination of alternative system 
concepts early in the process.  The task- based approach to manpower and training requirements 
determination also provides PMs with the flexibility to tailor the level of PMT analysis that will 
be applied to their program. 
 
40. Navy Officer Billet Classification.  A classification expressed as a 4-digit code, which 
identifies officer billet requirements and officer occupational experience, acquired through billet 
experience or a combination of education and experience. 
 
41. Navy Officer Occupational Classification System Board.  The Navy officer occupational 
classification system is the Navy's method to identify skills, education, training, experience and 
capabilities related to both officer personnel and manpower requirements.  This system forms the 
basis for officer personnel planning, manpower management, procurement, training, promotion, 
distribution, career development and mobilization.  The Navy officer occupational classification

42. NEC.  System of which NEC coding is a part.  It supplements the enlisted rating structure in 
identifying personnel on active or inactive duty and billets in manpower authorizations.  NEC 
codes identify a non-rating wide skill, knowledge, aptitude or qualification that must be 
documented to identify both people and billets for management purposes.  Additionally, an NEC 
can be used to identify special circumstances or situations with approval via the NEOCS process. 
 
43. NEOCS Board.  A method the Navy uses to identify enlisted personnel skills and the 
requirements associated with these skills.  The system forms the basis for actions taken 
concerning enlisted personnel planning, manpower management, procurement, training, 
advancement, promotion, distribution, assignment and mobilization.  The NEOCS board serves as 
the central point for changes to the NEOCS.  It reviews CPs in terms of overall system direction 
and makes recommendations as appropriate.  The board authorizes administrative actions for 
NEC changes and makes recommendations to CNO N1for enlisted rating structure changes. 
 
44. Non-Developmental Item.  Any hardware or software item that does not require 
development, such as commercially available items, items developed by other services or 
agencies or items developed by foreign governments with which the U.S. Government has a 
cooperation agreement.  Items requiring only minor modification to meet the established 
requirements of the procuring agency are also considered non-developmental items. 
 
45. NTSP.  The primary document for defining PMT requirements and resources for new 
systems development and modernization of existing systems.  The NTSP identifies the resources 
required to establish and maintain an effective training program throughout the lifecycle of the 
system.  Much like the system it addresses, the NTSP goes through periodic updates to reflect 
advances in technology to sustain and improve combat effectiveness.  The NTSP is a lifecycle 
document that identifies the resources required to establish and maintain an effective training 
program throughout the new systems' development and modernization.  It controls planning for 
meeting the training requirements of the system and identifies personnel required to install, 
operate, maintain or otherwise use the system.  It is a report that communicates the product 
support manager’s plan for formulating, implementing and executing a sustainment strategy for 
training.  It should communicate the strategy needed to develop and integrate sustainment 
requirements into system design, development, testing, fielding and operations or support.  
NTSPs are required for every Navy and integrated Navy and Marine Corps system. The 
approved NTSP is the official tasking document for establishing the system’s PMT requirements 
identified within the document. 
 
46. NTSP Tool.  Can be any process, program or software, websites or other method of 
producing NTSPs by a PM or a RS.

47. Participating Resource Manager.  An independent systems integrator who develops sub-
systems and equipment in parallel with ship construction to meet a defined in-yard date. 
 
48. PEO.  A military or civilian official who has primary responsibility for directing multiple 
ACAT I programs and for assigned ACAT II, III and IV programs.  A PEO has no other 
command or staff responsibilities within the component and only reports to and receives 
guidance and direction from the DoD component executive.  PEOs receive program functional 
support (engineering, logistics, contracting, accounting, legal, etc.) from SYSCOM commanders.  
Under the direction of the PEO, assigned PMs may assume the duties and responsibilities of PM, 
TSA and PM for system development, modernization and configuration management.  For PEOs 
responsible for ship and aircraft procurement, modernization, configuration management and 
lifecycle management, the PEO will coordinate with all PMs providing systems, subsystems and 
equipment to the ship and aircraft to ensure that training goals, objectives, responsibilities and 
schedules are defined. 
 
49. Performance Support. Designed as a “Point of Need” job performance aid in direct support of 
PQS, Planned Maintenance System documentation and infrequently performed tasks. 
 
50. PM.  A military or civilian official who is responsible for managing procurement, 
modernization and configuration management programs. 
 
51. RCDC.  A model that depicts a rating continuum showing the technical, professional (on-the-
job training, OCCSTD and PQS) and leadership throughout a career.  The Rating Continuum 
must be completed and approved as identified in enclosure (6). 
 
52. RDA.  Aligns all required training tasks to the proper point in a Sailor’s career continuum 
when they will be needed to perform their job and provides a recommendation for when training 
should be provided.  The analysis identifies and sequences the new or revised work a Sailor is 
expected to perform, and measures performance based on Fleet operational readiness standards 
for a given Rating Continuum.  The analysis will use a combination of AAE and Fleet 
performance data to inform the continuum and the timing of performance checks. 
 
53. RFT.  The date of a training system and its associated logistics, maintenance, syllabus and 
instructors are certified to be available for training at the learning facility.  This date is predicated 
on the availability of a new, modified or rehabilitated learning facility for training purposes.  All 
aspects of the facility must be ready, including building completion, completed site preparation, 
training system installation and testing, trained instructors, furnishings, e.g., non-technical 
collateral equipment.  This is the ultimate planning date for the new or modified training system 
and its readiness for use. 
 
54. RRL.  A pillar of Sailor 2025 that drives fundamental changes into our approach to Sailor 
training.  The goal is to provide the right training at the right time in the right  way to improve

relevance and accelerate our processes for delivering new training to the Fleet. 
 
55. RS.  The RS determines program objectives and time-phased support requirements and 
appraises programs, readiness and military worth for a given weapon system function or task in 
support of the goals and objectives.  The RS provides day-to-day OPNAV management of the 
assigned programs by acting as the central POC for the hardware systems coordinators and the 
PM.  An RS is an OPNAV principal official responsible for an identifiable aggregation of 
resources that constitute inputs to warfare and supporting warfare tasks. 
 
56. Simulation.  The imitation of the operation of a real-world process or system over time.  The 
act of simulating something first requires that a model be developed; this model represents the 
key characteristics or behaviors of the selected physical or abstract system or process.  The 
model represents the system itself, whereas the simulation represents the operation of the system 
over time. 
 
57. Software Acquisition Pathway.  Is for the timely procurement of custom software capabilities 
developed for the DoD.  Programs executing the software acquisition pathway are not subject to 
the JCIDS and will be handled as specifically provided for by the Vice Chairman of the Joint 
Chiefs of Staff, in consultation with Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment 
and each service executive.  Programs using the software acquisition pathway will demonstrate 
the viability and effectiveness of capabilities for operational use not later than 1 year after the 
date on which funds are first obligated to develop the new software capability.  New capabilities 
will be delivered to operations at least annually to iteratively meet requirements, but more 
frequent updates and deliveries are encouraged where practical.  For programs using the 
embedded software path, this annual update applies after initial operational acceptance of the 
system in which the software is embedded and should be aligned with the associated system’s 
schedule.  Before the operational acceptance of the system in which the software is embedded, 
software deliveries will be delivered to an operationally representative environment at least 
annually. 
 
58. Student Profile.  The term includes the student being trained, the learning from the interactive 
courseware, including course and training description, periodicity, course number, reference and 
training points.  Used to construct learning situations to acquire the knowledge and skills 
required for the accomplishment of specific tasks. 
 
59. SYSCOM.  The command responsible for implementing policy and procedures for the 
execution of assigned procurement and modernization programs.  They manage the logistics 
support plan through their functional and program management organizations. 
 
60. System.  A grouping of functionally related subsystems and equipment operating together to 
support a major function or meet a tactical purpose.  The term system includes subsystems, a

61. TA.  An office, bureau, command or headquarters exercising command of and providing 
support to some major increment of the DON’s formalized training effort.  Depending upon the 
level of oversight required, the TA may be NETC, a Learning Center or other designated 
organizations. 
 
62. Task Analysis.  Identifies the tasks required for performance on the job that are analyzed for 
how a task is accomplished, task and element durations, task frequency, task allocation, task 
complexity, task criticality, environmental conditions, equipment and any other unique factors 
involved in or required to perform a task into a Master Task List.  Using the Master Task List 
further task analysis is conducted to identify, using various factors, tasks that require training 
into a Training Task List. 
 
63. TD.  Hardware and software which have been designed or modified exclusively for training 
purposes involving, to some degree, simulation or stimulation in its construction or operation, to 
demonstrate or illustrate a concept or simulate an operational environment. 
 
64. TDCP.  A document used to identify and evaluate alternative approaches to the design of the 
training system and to recommend the best approach. 
 
65. TE.  An authoritative source for out-year projections of training requirements and 
assessments.  The TE is prepared by the PM and approved by RS(s). A MS B product developed 
using MTRP data.  The TE consists of enclosure (3) parts I, V, VI and VII.  The TE will be 
aligned with the manpower estimate or preliminary manpower documents (or both).  The 
analysis will be updated and transitioned into an NTSP at MS C. 
 
66. TITA.  Defines how the Navy coordinates the design, installation and incorporation of a 
training system and transfer to a schoolhouse.  A properly developed agreement: (1) defines 
facility requirements, including MILCON and special project planning, for installation of the 
training system; (2) identifies all associated logistics support elements; and (3) transfers training 
ownership responsibility from the TSA to the TA.  The agreement is an integral part of the 
system process as it supports the introduction of new training systems and equipment, 
incorporates training system changes and identifies sustainment requirements.  Close liaison 
between the TSA and TA are key to implementing training system installation plans that meet an 
RFT date. 
 
67. TLCE.  Assessment of the estimated cost of developing, producing, deploying, maintaining, 
operating and disposing of a system over its entire lifespan. 
 
68. Training Materials.  See curricula materials.

70. TSA.  The office, bureau, command or headquarters responsible for supporting the TA by 
providing material and other forms of support within the cognizance of the office, bureau or 
command involved.  The TSA provides initial training for the ESS until the TA can acquire the 
capability for training.  The TSA is an activity with fiscal responsibility for supporting the TA by

headquarters involved.  The TSA will be the PM who is assigned funding responsibility for all 
investments and expense costs required to turn over a training system end item and allow the TA 
to sustain the capability for training. 
 
71. TSD.  The TSD is a DID DI-SESS-81517C that documents the efficiency of a training 
system to meet training needs and to survey training programs and technologies for applicability 
to new training needs.  It is not intended that all the requirements contained within the DID be 
applied to every program or program phase.  Any data requirement contained in this DID is 
subject to deletion tailoring.

73. TSPO.  Refer to section 6(g) in main policy for each TSPO definition. 
 
74. TTE.  Investment cost end items of operational equipment, devoted to the training and 
instruction of naval personnel, for which PMs have responsibility for design, development, 
modernization, configuration management or selection for service or special use. 
 
75. Urgent Need.  An urgent need is an unusual request from a Navy or Marine Corps 
component commander for an additional warfighting capability critically needed by operating 
forces conducting combat or contingency operations.  Failure to deliver the capability requested 
is likely to result in the inability of units to accomplish their missions or increases the probability 
of casualties and loss of life.  The DON's urgent need process encompasses Navy urgent 
operational need, Marine Corps urgent universal need statement and joint urgent operational 
needs assigned to the DON.
